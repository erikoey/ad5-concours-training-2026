To: Director-General, Eurostat
Subject: Meeting with the Internal Audit Service — statistical peer reviews

Purpose
Prepare your discussion of the Court’s peer-review findings in Special Report 26/2022. Acknowledge progress while focusing on follow-up, coverage and independent oversight.

Main findings
The Commission made good progress on most recommendations from Eurostat’s 2014 review, especially legislative architecture, communication and users. However, key independence and impartiality recommendations were not accepted or effectively addressed. These concerned senior-management appointment and dismissal arrangements and privileged pre-release access. Progress elsewhere should not be presented as resolving these concerns. (Paragraphs 70–75)

Eurostat’s monitoring of national improvement actions relied mainly on annual questionnaires and mutual trust. Clarification requests to verify reported progress were introduced only in the final 2019 monitoring round. At the end of 2019, 155 of 910 actions (17%) remained incomplete. Among professional-independence actions, 25 of 72 (35%) were outstanding. Some delays depended on legislation, political support or resources outside statistical institutes’ control. Relevant ministries and parliaments were not always involved early enough. (Paragraphs 76–82)

Review design improved: the third round covered all 16 Code principles and combined peer-review and audit-like approaches. However, the average gap between the second and third rounds was seven and a half years, too long for continuous improvement. The ceiling of six other national authorities did not reflect differences in decentralisation. Selection criteria were insufficiently comparable and national statistical institutes selected the participating bodies. (Paragraphs 83–87)

Despite its independence from national interests, ESGAB has a limited role in design decisions. Recommendation 5 asks the Commission to assess the feasibility and appropriateness of extending ESGAB’s mandate to deciding all key review-design elements, with a 2023 target. This is a request for an assessment, not an immediate transfer of responsibilities. (Paragraphs 88–89, 101–103; Recommendation 5)

Proposed next steps — suggestions, not agreed commitments
1. Use clearer evidence requirements and proportionate verification, prioritising overdue actions and independence risks.
2. Identify actions requiring legislative, political or funding decisions; involve the responsible actors earlier.
3. Examine more timely reviews and consistent, risk-based selection of other national authorities.
4. Organise the ESGAB feasibility assessment, including governance, legal and resource implications.

Speaking points
• “The Court recognises progress. We should build on it while addressing the remaining weaknesses.”
• “Reported completion should be supported by evidence of effective implementation, especially on independence.”
• “Where reforms depend on ministries or legislators, earlier engagement and clear ownership are essential.”
• “Coverage should reflect national systems’ risks and structure, rather than arbitrary numerical limits.”
• “We should assess the benefits and practical implications of a stronger ESGAB role.”
• “I would welcome your views on stronger assurance without duplicating controls.”
